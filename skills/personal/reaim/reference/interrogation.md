# Interrogating the goal fields

How to get a `why`, `anti_goals`, `plan`, `crystal_ball`, `remedies` and
`system` out of a person, and how to tell when what you got is hollow.

These are **procedural**, like the four in `constraints.md`: they govern the
conversation, not the artifact. The contract in `weekly-cycle-model` says what a
goal must carry; this says how to ask, and what the common failure looks like
when it arrives dressed as a real answer.

## What is contract and what is method

Only `plan` is in the global contract, because without it the estimate has no
subject. The other five fields here are **this method's**, not the loop's. A
consumer that wants different fields points `goals.format` at its own file and
`goals.constraints` at its own discipline, and the same facades run unchanged.

That split is deliberate. The loop is meant to give structure to someone who
wants rhythm and direction; six mandatory fields per goal is not a light start.
GPS is the default because it is good, not because the machinery needs it.

Source: Ali Abdaal's GPS method. Quotes below are his words; the failure modes
are what this practice has actually produced.

## The rule the whole thing hangs on

**Estimate the plan, not the goal.** Both axes take the plan as their subject:

> "assuming we followed the plan to 100% accuracy, would we actually get to the
> goal" — theory
> "will I actually follow the plan" — practice

A goal with no written plan cannot be estimated, only guessed at. One cycle
scored a goal at practice 85 because the *goal* was trivial: speak aloud,
material already written. The *plan* was fragile, with no tracking, no
accountability and no protected block. It registered zero. Estimating the goal
hid the diagnosis.

When a field below is hollow, that shows up as a practice score too high to be
useful. The interrogation is what keeps the number honest.

## Which fields to interrogate, and when

Not every field earns a fresh round every cycle. Ali budgets five to ten minutes
per goal; six full interrogations per goal blows past that, and a ceremony that
costs too much stops being run.

| Field | When |
|---|---|
| `why` | Once, when the goal is born. Carried goals inherit it. |
| `anti_goals` | Once, same. Revisit only if the goal's scope changes. |
| `plan` | **Every cycle.** It is what gets estimated. |
| `crystal_ball` | **Every cycle.** Obstacles are specific to the week ahead. |
| `remedies` | **Every cycle.** They answer this cycle's obstacles. |
| `system` | **Every cycle**, but briefly. Usually "same as last week" is true and fine. |

A carried goal whose `why` still reads true does not get re-interrogated. Ask
once, "does this still hold?", and move on.

---

## `why`

**Ask:** why does this matter to you, not why it should matter.

**The failure:** the *should*. Ali:

> "A lot of us choose goals from a sense of should or a sense of obligation."

An extrinsic `why` fills the field perfectly and predicts nothing. His evidence
is that extrinsic motivation makes you both less likely to hit the goal and less
likely to enjoy hitting it.

**The contrast:** he does not write "health". He writes family history of heart
disease, South Asian genetics, DEXA scans showing visceral fat, a newborn
daughter, and not wanting to die of a myocardial infarction while she is small.

**The test:** if the `why` would serve somebody else's version of this goal
unchanged, it is not finished.

## `anti_goals`

**Ask:** what will you not sacrifice to get this?

**The failure:** the preference wearing a constraint's clothes. A goal to sell
part of a portfolio once named the distribution as an anti-goal, then added "a
preference, not a constraint: the sale happens anyway". That is honest labelling
of something that does not belong in the field. A constraint that yields under
pressure constrains nothing.

**The contrast:** Ali will not run in the evenings, because that is family time.
The constraint removes an otherwise-obvious option from the plan. That is the
signal: an anti-goal you can satisfy without changing the plan was never binding.

**The test:** does it rule something out? If the plan is identical with and
without it, it is prose, not an anti-goal.

**Worth saying plainly:** an empty `anti_goals` is a fine answer. A fabricated
one is not, and per `constraints.md` §1, never invent one to fill the field.

## `plan`

**Ask:** what are the three to five major moves that get you there?

**The failure:** a plan that does not reach the goal even if followed perfectly.
Ali's example is the person wanting $10k/month passive income whose plan is
posting on Instagram weekly and chasing brand deals for six months. Followed to
the letter it does not arrive: wrong cadence, wrong platform, and brand deals are
not passive income.

**The second failure:** a plan nobody would follow. Running five hours a day
would produce the caloric deficit. It will not happen.

Those two failures are exactly what theory and practice separate, which is why
the plan has to exist *before* the estimate rather than after.

**The test:** read the plan aloud and ask "if I did all of this, does the goal
happen?" Then "will I do all of this?" If either makes you hesitate, the number
you are about to write is already known.

## `crystal_ball`

**Ask:** it is Friday and this did not happen. What are the top three reasons?

This is Gabriele Oettingen's mental contrasting. Ali:

> "You're sort of predicting what obstacles you're gonna run into before you're
> getting started."

**The failure:** generic obstacles. "I might get busy" is true of every goal in
every week and therefore forecasts nothing. Ali's are specific and drawn from
past attempts: blowing past 2200 calories, missing 150g of protein and losing
muscle, not hitting 10,000 steps.

**The prompt that fixes it:** "when this failed before, what actually happened?"
Past failure is the best available forecast, and a carried goal has recorded
failures to mine.

**The test:** could this reason appear verbatim under a different goal? Then it
is not yet this goal's obstacle.

## `remedies`

**Ask:** what mechanism stops each of those?

**The failure:** the intention. "I'll be more careful with calories" is not a
remedy. This is the field where effort masquerades as design most easily.

**The contrast:** Ali answers "how do I stay under 2200 calories?" with *track on
MyFitnessPal*, and "how do I get 10,000 steps when busy?" with a walking
treadmill under the desk, so the steps accumulate during calls he was having
anyway. One changes the instrument, the other changes the environment.

**The test:** a remedy names a tool, a time, a person, a removed dependency, or a
changed default. If it starts with "I'll try to", it is an intention.

This is the same standard `setting-cycle-goals` already applies to `support`:

> "Make time for it" is not support; "Tuesday 9-12 blocked, phone in a drawer" is.

A remedy is support aimed at a forecast obstacle. Same bar.

## `system`

Three parts, and the third is the one most practices never have.

**`tracking` — ask:** how will you see progress without going looking for it?

Ali cites a meta-analysis: 138 studies, nearly 20,000 participants, monitoring
progress predicts achieving it. The mechanism is not discipline, it is exposure.
Seeing a sleep score of 64 nudges the next decision without deliberate effort.

**The failure:** tracking that costs more than the action. If it takes longer to
record than to do, it stops by Wednesday. Frictionless beats thorough.

**`reminders` — ask:** what puts this in front of you on a day you have
forgotten it?

Ali's own diagnosis is the sharpest evidence here:

> "The runs should be more systemized for me because they're currently not. I
> kind of make time for running when I have the time which is why I'm a lot less
> consistent with this than I am with the gym where it's actually in the
> calendar."

Same person, same goal, same motivation. The only variable is the calendar block.

**`accountability` — ask:** who is expecting this, and when will they notice it
is missing?

**The failure:** nobody, silently. A CV goal once failed three cycles running
with no one expecting anything. The cycle that finally named a specific person,
already informed, who would notice, is what a real answer looks like.

**The test:** accountability is real when somebody other than you would be
surprised by the failure.

## When a field stays empty

Empty is allowed. Fabricated is not, and `constraints.md` §1 governs: raise it as
a question, never slip it in.

But an empty field is evidence about the estimate. A goal with no accountability,
no tracking and no reminder has a practice score that should reflect that, and
the gate in `setting-cycle-goals` is where it gets paid for. The interrogation
adds no gate; it makes the existing one honest.
